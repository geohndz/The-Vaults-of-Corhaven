    Rules

    Goal. Be the first player to move all four of your troops from your keep into your vault.

    Turn order. At the start of a match, every player rolls both dice. The highest total goes first, then the next highest, and so on. Players who tie roll again.

    Two dice. Each die is a separate move. Use them on two different troops, or on the same troop one after the other.

    Leaving the keep. A die showing 1 or 6 moves a troop out of your keep onto your gate, the colored square next to your castle. Enemy troops on your gate are captured when you move out.

    Capturing. Land exactly on a square with a single enemy troop to capture it. The captured troop goes back to its keep, and you get a bonus move of 20 squares for any troop that can use it.

    Shrines and gates. Shrines are the grey squares with the shrine mark. Troops on a shrine can't be captured, so troops of different kingdoms can share one. You can only stop on another kingdom's gate when it's empty.

    Phalanx. Two troops of the same kingdom on one square form a phalanx. No troop can pass through it or land on it, including your own. Troops of different kingdoms never form a phalanx. A square holds at most two troops.

    Doubles. Rolling doubles gives you another roll after you move. If two of your troops form a phalanx, one of them moves first with the double, before you choose the other die. Rolling doubles three times in a row ends your turn, and your troop furthest along goes back to the keep.

    The road and the vault. Troops move counterclockwise around the 68-square road, then turn into their own passage: the seven squares in their kingdom's color that lead to the vault. Entering the vault takes an exact roll and earns a bonus move of 20 squares.

    Winning. The first player to get all four troops into their vault wins. In ranked matches, the others keep playing for second and third place.

    Fate Cards. Landing on a shrine deals you four Fate Cards, and you keep one. Blessings and Tactical cards help you. Curses work against you. You can hold two cards at a time, so with a full hand you discard one to make room. Each card shows what it does and how long it lasts.

    Mist. After every third round, Mist covers the board for one round. You can always see your own troops. Enemy troops on the road are hidden unless they're on or next to a square with one of your troops, and move previews won't reveal them.

    Classic. Classic matches have no Mist and no Fate Cards. The board is plain, with no castles, treasure or ruins, and shrines are the grey squares with a star. Every other rule is the same.

    Conquest. Conquest has no vault, Mist, Fate Cards or phalanxes. The passages are closed, so troops keep circling the road. Shrines are the grey squares with a star. Landing on a square captures every enemy troop there, unless it's a shrine or a gate. When a troop leaves your keep and another of your troops is on the gate, the troop on the gate moves one square ahead. A captured troop joins your keep and marches for you, and it wears a band in its first kingdom's color. A kingdom falls when it holds none of its four original troops. Any troops it still has join the kingdom that captured the last one. The last kingdom remaining wins.

    Keyboard: Space rolls the dice, the Left and Right arrows choose a die, and 1 to 4 move a troop.

    About the Vaults of Corhaven

    The chronicle

    Five kingdoms once shared the realm, and one of them ruled the rest. From its white towers at the heart of the land, Corhaven took tribute from the four kingdoms around it: iron from Emberhold, timber from Thornwald, grain from Solmere and salt from Tidereach. All of it was locked away in the vaults beneath the city.

    In time the four refused to pay. They rose together, broke Corhaven's walls and burned its streets. Then they turned on one another, because each meant to rule in Corhaven's place. The war lasted three hundred years and none of them won. The city was fought over so many times that nothing was left standing, and its vaults vanished under the rubble.

    At last, among the ruins, the four monarchs signed the Pact of Four Stones. The realm was divided into four equal kingdoms, and the land around the dead city became the Sundered March, a no man's land no army would ever enter again. If no kingdom could grow, none could rule the others.

    The peace held for a hundred years, and still the March did not heal. Storms came down under clear skies, rivers rose and swallowed whole roads in a night, and unnatural mists hid the stones from anyone a few paces off. The kingdoms left it alone. It was a scar on the map, and a warning, until the vaults were worth the risk.

    Then, over four nights, a hooded rider came to each court in turn with the same message. The vaults of Corhaven survived. A hidden passage runs from each kingdom's old road down to the gold, and whoever reaches it first will be rich enough to rule the realm without lifting a sword.

    Each monarch burned the letter, swore their council to silence and sent their troops to the border keeps.

    Each believed they were the only one who knew.

    The kingdoms

    Emberhold

    Emberhold's tribute was iron, and the keep still smells of the forge. Its gate is the colour of a cooling brand. The troops who leave it march for the passage that runs back under their own walls, and for the gold that was smelted from their hills.

    Thornwald

    Thornwald paid in timber. The forest has taken back most of the old cuts, and the roads out of the green keep are narrower than the maps say. Thornwald wants the vault because a kingdom that holds Corhaven's gold can buy what the trees no longer give.

    Solmere

    Solmere's fields paid Corhaven in grain. The harvest still comes, and the court still eats well, which is why the other kingdoms trust Solmere least. A full store and a secret passage are enough to outlast a siege without ever fighting one.

    Tidereach

    Tidereach's tribute was salt, taken from the reaches where the rivers go brackish. The blue keep watches the water the way Emberhold watches the fire. If the vaults open, Tidereach means to hold the roads that feed them, and to charge every wagon that passes.

    The Sundered March

    The March is the ring of land around the dead city. No kingdom claims it. The Pact of Four Stones forbade armies from entering, and for a hundred years they obeyed, because the ground itself did not.

    The old King's Road still runs there: sixty-eight squares around the ruins, counted the long way, past shrines where no blood may be spilled and past the four gates that open back into the keeps. Each kingdom's hidden passage leaves that road and drops seven squares to its own vault.

    The road does not stay quiet. Every third time the kingdoms have all marched, a mist comes down over the March and hides the road beyond a few paces. It lifts once every kingdom has marched again. The troops are already on the road when it happens. They march through it.

    The kings

    Four monarchs signed the Pact, and four sit the keeps now. None of them has said the rider's message aloud.

    King Halric of Emberhold

    Halric keeps the forges lit whether or not there is an order to fill. He burned the rider's letter in the mouth of a furnace and told his council the iron would be enough. He has sent four troops to the border anyway.

    Queen Briar of Thornwald

    Briar read the letter under the trees and burned it there, where the smoke would look like a clearing fire. She has not told the court what the rider said. The troops she sent out were ordered to gather timber. They were not given saws.

    King Oswin of Solmere

    Oswin smiled at the rider, fed him, and burned the letter with the kitchen coals. Solmere can afford to wait. He would rather the other kingdoms spend their troops on the road while his grain keeps, and his own four riders go out looking like a tax party.

    Queen Lirra of Tidereach

    Lirra took the letter to the salt stairs, where the wind pulls paper apart, and let it go. She has posted her troops on the roads that lead into the March and called it a watch against smugglers. The vault, if it is there, will have to pay her to pass.

    The Veil of Mist

    An unnatural fog hides everything beyond a short distance. In play, it covers the board after every third round and clears after one round. While it lasts, you can see all of your own troops, but enemy troops on the road only when they're on or next to a square with one of yours.
