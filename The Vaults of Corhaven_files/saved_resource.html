Rules of the realm

Who rides first. Each kingdom rolls both dice at the start. The highest total moves first, the way the rider reached that court first, and so on down. Ties roll again.

Two dice. Each die is its own march. Use them on two different troops, or on the same troop one after the other.

Leaving the keep. A die showing 1 or 6 sends a troop out through your gate, the coloured square beside your castle. Enemy troops standing at your gate fall when you march out.

Falling in battle. Land exactly on a lone enemy troop and it falls. Its kingdom must raise a new troop at its keep. The victor presses on with a bonus march of 20 squares for any troop that can make it.

Shrines and gates. Grey star squares are shrines where no blood may be spilled, so troops of different kingdoms can stand there together. You may only stop on another kingdom's gate when nobody is standing on it.

Phalanx. Two troops of the same kingdom on one square lock shields into a phalanx. Nobody may pass or land on it. Troops of different kingdoms never form a phalanx. A square holds at most two troops.

Doubles. Rolling doubles lets you roll again after marching. A third double in a row is greed punished: your furthest troop is lost and must be raised again at the keep, and your turn ends.

The passages. Troops march counterclockwise around the 68 squares of the old King's Road, then into their own kingdom's hidden passage, the seven coloured squares, to the vault. Reaching the vault needs an exact count and earns a bonus march of 20.

Victory. The first kingdom to bring all four troops to the vault claims the gold of Corhaven.

Keys: Space rolls, left and right arrows pick a die, 1 to 4 move a troop.

The Chronicle of Corhaven

Five kingdoms once shared the realm, and one of them ruled the rest. From its white towers at the heart of the land, Corhaven took tribute from the four kingdoms around it: iron from Emberhold, timber from Thornwald, grain from Solmere and salt from Tidereach. All of it was locked away in the vaults beneath the city.

In time the four refused to pay. They rose together, broke Corhaven's walls and burned its streets. Then they turned on one another, because each meant to rule in Corhaven's place. The war lasted three hundred years and none of them won. The city was fought over so many times that nothing was left standing, and its vaults vanished under the rubble.

At last, among the ruins, the four monarchs signed the Pact of Four Stones. The realm was divided into four equal kingdoms, and the land around the dead city became the Sundered March, a no man's land no army would ever enter again. If no kingdom could grow, none could rule the others.

The peace held for a hundred years.

Then, over four nights, a hooded rider came to each court in turn with the same message. The vaults of Corhaven survived. A hidden passage runs from each kingdom's old road down to the gold, and whoever reaches it first will be rich enough to rule the realm without lifting a sword.

Each monarch burned the letter, swore their council to silence and sent their troops to the border keeps.

Each believed they were the only one who knew.
